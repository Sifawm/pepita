# 07 · Gamification and rewards

**Principles from the book it touches:** 4th law (immediate reward), 2nd law (make it attractive, **temptation bundling**), the warning that extrinsic reward should lead to intrinsic reward (identity).

## How each app handles it

| App | Level | Mechanics |
|---|---|---|
| **Habitica** | **Maximum** | Full role-playing game: XP, levels, gold, HP, mana, classes (Warrior, Mage, Rogue, Healer), pets and mounts, gear, boss quests **as a party**, achievements, events. **Custom Rewards bought with gold**: the closest thing to temptation bundling. Paid gems |
| **Finch** | **High, emotional** | Completing goals gives **energy**; the pet needs a daily minimum to go on adventures and grow. The currency (*Rainbow Stones*) buys clothes and furniture. Seasonal events, monthly challenges. No punishment |
| **Fabulous** | Medium, narrative | Journeys as illustrated chapters, letters, celebrations |
| **Routinery** | Low | A plant that grows with the streak |
| **Atoms** | Low, sensory | Animation, vibration and sound on logging. Milestones. *Perfect Days* |
| **Strides** | Low | *Perfect day*, shareable celebrations |
| **Streaks** | Low | Streaks, 30+ themes, gold theme if you're "on pace" |
| **Habitify** | Low | Confetti on completing the day, milestones |
| **Productive** | Low | Milestone rewards, *perfect days* |
| **Loop** | Minimal | Confetti |
| **Beeminder / stickK** | None | The engine is loss aversion |
| **Way of Life, HabitNow, TickTick, HabitShare** | None or minimal | Themes, shareable image |

## Market patterns

- There are two ways to gain a lot of retention through gamification:
  1. **Role-playing game with social pressure** (Habitica): works for ADHD profiles and students, but has a steep learning curve and the punishment causes anxiety.
  2. **Compassionate pet** (Finch): 4.9★ with about 777,000 ratings. Criticism: "I check tasks just for the rewards", and there's no difficulty progression.
- The "serious" apps stick to **immediate sensory feedback** (Atoms) and milestones.

## Gaps and opportunities

- **Explicit temptation bundling**: pair a habit with something you enjoy ("I only listen to my podcast while I walk"). Nobody models it; Habitica comes close with its *Rewards*.
- A **substitution reward** for habits being broken (the book: "open an account to buy yourself something with what you save").
- If there's gamification, the **game should reflect the identity** and not replace it (Finch's risk).
