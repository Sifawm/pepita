# 11 · Reflection, journaling and mood

**Principles from the book it touches:** **review and reflection** (annual review, integrity review), habit scorecard (becoming aware), identifying the cues of bad habits.

## How each app handles it

| App | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Finch** | **Morning mood check-in and evening gratitude and reflection**, with guided journaling prompts. Insights that cross mood, tags, goals and questionnaires |
| **Fabulous** | Journal, gratitude journal, mood, affirmations |
| **Way of Life** | **Journal per day or per habit**: what went well, **what triggered the bad habit**. Exports to JSON |
| **Habitify** | Note + mood on each log, *Reflections* gathering all notes, searchable Journal |
| **stickK** | *Commitment Journal* (notes, photos, mood, setbacks) visible to the referee and supporters |
| **Atoms** | "Reflections" tied to the identity (reviews say they lack guidance) |
| **TickTick** | Note + mood emoji when logging |
| **Productive** | Notes; reflections inside *Programs* |
| **Loop, Streaks, Strides, Beeminder** | Notes per log |
| **Routinery** | Emotion log |
| **Habitica, HabitNow, HabitShare** | Notes or comments |

## Market patterns

- The usual thing is **a note or a mood emoji when logging**.
- Guided reflection (Finch, Fabulous) is associated with wellbeing apps more than with trackers.
- Way of Life has an idea closely aligned with the book: **note what triggered the bad habit**.

## Gaps and opportunities

- **Note the reason for the failure** with categories tied to the 4 laws (I didn't notice / I didn't feel like it / it was hard / it didn't feel worth it). Feeds the diagnosis (see doc 09).
- A 5-minute **guided weekly review** and a periodic **integrity review** of the identities.
- A **habit scorecard** as an initial and recurring exercise.
