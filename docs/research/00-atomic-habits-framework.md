# 00 · Reference framework: *Atomic Habits* (James Clear)

Base document. Each feature document (01–14) states which principle of the book it touches. That way we can see which of the book's ideas the apps on the market already cover and which remain uncovered.

## Core ideas

| Concept | What the book says | Implication for an app |
|---|---|---|
| **1% better** | Small changes compound; results lag behind effort. | Show the accumulated trend, not just today. |
| **Plateau of latent potential** | For weeks progress isn't visible, and that's when most people give up. | Feedback is needed that makes invisible progress visible: accumulated votes, habit strength. |
| **Systems > goals** | Goals set the direction; the system is what produces progress. | Goals exist, but the core of the app is the daily process. |
| **Identity-based habits** | Three layers: outcomes → processes → identity. Every action is a *vote* for the type of person you want to be. | Every habit hangs from an identity ("I'm a person who reads"). Every check-in counts as a vote. |
| **Habit loop** | Cue → craving → response → reward. | Each phase has its law and its inverse (below). |

## The 4 laws (building) and their inverses (breaking)

| Phase | Law to build | Techniques from the book | Inverse to break |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cue | **1. Make it obvious** | *Habit Scorecard*, **implementation intention** ("I will do X at time Y in place Z"), **stacking** ("After [current habit], I will do [new one]"), environment design | **Make it invisible**: remove cues from the environment |
| Craving | **2. Make it attractive** | **Temptation bundling**, joining a culture where that behavior is the norm, motivation ritual beforehand, reframing ("I have to" → "I get to") | **Make it unattractive**: highlight the benefits of avoiding it |
| Response | **3. Make it easy** | **Two-minute rule**, reduce friction, prime the environment, "decisive moments", master the minimum version before scaling, automate | **Make it difficult**: more friction, **commitment device** |
| Reward | **4. Make it satisfying** | Immediate reward, **habit tracking** ("don't break the chain"), **never miss twice**, reinforcement | **Make it unsatisfying**: **accountability partner**, **habit contract** with consequences |

## Concrete techniques that can become features

1. **Habit scorecard**: list your current habits and mark them as +, − or =. A starting point to become aware.
2. **Implementation intention**: when and where, as structured fields.
3. **Habit stacking**: anchor a new habit to an existing one (chains or routines).
4. **Environment design**: prepare the space ("leave your sneakers by the door"). It would be a preparation checklist.
5. **Temptation bundling**: pair something you *need* to do with something you *want* to do.
6. **Two-minute rule**: every habit has a minimum version that takes 2 minutes or less.
7. **Scaling with the Goldilocks rule**: difficulty at the edge of your ability. Increase it progressively.
8. **Habit tracking**: immediate visual logging. The book recommends tracking only what matters and automating logging when possible.
9. **Never miss twice**: one miss is an accident, two is the start of a new habit.
10. **Habit contract**: a written commitment with consequences and witnesses.
11. **Accountability partner**: someone who sees whether you follow through.
12. **Review and reflection**: annual and integrity review (am I still living according to my values and identity?).

## Warnings from the book itself (useful for design)

- Danger of the measurement becoming the goal (Goodhart's law). The streak isn't the end.
- Habit tracking must be **immediate** and **simple**. If logging takes effort, people don't log.
- Once you master a habit, it becomes boring. The challenge becomes falling in love with boredom.
- Identity can become rigid. It's worth reviewing it every now and then.
